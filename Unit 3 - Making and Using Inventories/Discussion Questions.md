# Unit 3 Discussion Post 1: Find a blog post or open source tool that focuses on IT inventory management

    1. What is meant by the term “inventory” in an IT context?

```
        A list or accounting of some type or category of item. 
```

    2. What are some of the issues with inventories in a company?

```
        They aren't kept up to date.  They are tracked by different people or workgroups within the organization.
```

    3. How have people attempted to overcome these issues?

```
        Everyone since the dawn of time has fought this battle. The ones that have survived were probably taken by some greybeard wizard who used CSV or markdown.
```

    4. What different formats of inventories can you find for IT management?

```
        It depends on the process by which it was taken.  It might be a text list or a spreadsheet. It could be some DB that was generated by a specific piece of software. 
```

    5. Why would formatting matter?

```
        If it was taken by some automated means and now that software is out of date or unsupported, you've lost your data. Lots of problems can arise from not using standardized formats.
```

# Unit 3 Discussion Post 2: You are a system administrator for a small company with ~100 total Linux systems. The security engineer approaches you and shows you vulnerabilities in your 110 total Linux systems. He then asserts, “without a good inventory, you cannot have security in the system.”

1. Do you agree with him, why or why not?

```
Without inventories, organizations don't know what assets need protection. Identifying all hardware and software is a prerequisite for building a modern defense strategy that can manage risk and respond to incidents.
Also, properly managed and standardized systems are more cost effective to manage from an IT perspective. Imagine walking into a small business in the mid 90s and finding no two computers that even looked alike. Imagine having to help Alice configure her email on a non-standard PC in a non-standard world. Certain norms today are taken for granted but there is always an interesting story behind why the rules were created in the first place. 

Developing an asset inventory is a multi-step process where OT owners and operators identify, classify, and document assets. OT owners and operators that develop an OT taxonomy as part of the inventory process can significantly enhance the process. 
```

2. How do you plan to start to “true up” your inventories?

```
You can start today. Open a text editor and start writing. Make the first edition master copy and go from there.  Sometimes you have to start from scratch but you should never end up doing it twice. (This IS an automation class after all)
```

3. How can you prevent this type of problem (if you think it is one) in the future?

```
See 2 above. Create a solution moving forward.
```

4. We often say in engineering, “Or you can do nothing”. This speaks to the possibility of just accepting the situation and allowing a system to keep running.

```
Accepting the situation is not acceptable.  Even if it's running perfectly, it probably isn't. How will you ever know?
```

5. Can you do that in this situation, or must this be corrected? Why or why not?

```
Creating a system or method, and gaining a controllable inventory is inevitably necessary but the time sensitivity is subjective. While not a security discussion, this is actually a prime example of why it's necessary to maintain knowledge and organization of your assets. 

An excerpt from CISA’s Cybersecurity Performance Goals...
Creating an asset inventory is necessary for building a modern defensible architecture and one of CISA’s Cybersecurity Performance Goals. A modern defensible architecture mitigates risk through a thoughtful system design and implementation that enables OT cyber defenders to identify, prevent, and respond to cyber threats while ensuring reliability, operational continuity, safety, and compliance with regulatory requirements.

```
