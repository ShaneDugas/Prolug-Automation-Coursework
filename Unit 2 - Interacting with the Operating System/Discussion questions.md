This has been an interesting exercise to contemplate. 
After you get past the debates on whether its true for this subject or not true for another, the question inevitably arises in one's mind.  Why is it a thing we are still talking about and why has it persisted?

As a generalistic idea, something taken from 40-50k ft view, it's there.  It shows up over and over again in many facets of the world around us.  We can debate on the validity of 80/20 all day. Then we can move on to Fibonacci in nature. (all good debates to have at some point - for any curious mind) But if we just think about it "As a wide brush"...

The next question that kept returning to me was around "How can I use this?"  How can I use automation to "show me" the bulk of the things I need to steer around, avoid, fix.  Not some specific command for some specific thing.  How can I use "a wide brush" or "cast a big net" to find 80% of  the  things that are going to show up as a problem later. Something like (S)cott mentioned yesterday, as a high level query, "What servers are even up?" You might have 8 flavors to deal with but the ones that are down are the ones you deal with first.  This will be something that I will try to keep in the back of my mind as the course progresses.

The gist of the message was that choosing speed over completeness in terms of over-arching direction in the industry can be really advantageous.  We will never get everything perfect and if we are intentful to not get hung up on the minutia, it can pay off in terms of speed.  The whole statement was very apropos in terms of 80/20.
